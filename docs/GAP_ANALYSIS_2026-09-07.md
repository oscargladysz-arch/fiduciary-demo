## 1. Bottom line

Round 2 landed. The rubric no longer scores strategies, the peer composite is no longer called a PME, the false affiliations are gone, the dead accession is fixed, the verbatim rule text is in the panel and the memo, the memos are per plan without cross-plan leaks, the forms hand the person a valid file, a human signature now passes the gate, and the deploy is recorded with an EDGAR check you ran yourself. The record and the engines are, for the first time, defensible in substance. That is the important sentence in this document.

What is left is a different kind of problem, and it is the one you named: the site is unsightly, and more than unsightly, it is built in a way that fights the user. Concretely:

1. The browser Back button leaves the site. Routing uses `history.replaceState` only (`site/js/main.js:102`), so a first-time adviser who clicks three views and presses Back is returned to their email client.
2. Every click re-renders the entire app from strings, scrolls to the top, and drops focus and form state (`main.js:285-303`). Changing one Screener filter resets scroll from 172 to 0 and focus to `body`. Typing an advisor statement and then toggling density erased it. A liquidity slider moved to 40% reset to 20% on any header action.
3. The landing page breaks its own promise. The Screener tagline says "Every value clicks through to its citation," but a fresh load renders 0 citation buttons, because `cell.source` ships only in the lazy `series.js` that the Screener never requests (`views.js:174-178`, `build_site.py:628-633`). After visiting any other view, 286 buttons appear. Reproduced live on the landing URL.
4. Thirty-seven coverage donuts are invisible: the Roster's 16 rings and Coverage's 17 render as 0 by 0 SVGs (`charts.js:252-253`, `views.js:377, 1684, 1702`).
5. Type and contrast fail the guidelines you adopted: 20 distinct font sizes, 33 to 49 percent of text nodes under 12 px on the six main views, minimum 9.5 px, and the caption color `--ink-3 #837b8e` at 4.05:1 on white (AA needs 4.5) on 99 to 156 nodes per view. Zero author focus styles, zero `aria-live`, three `aria-*` attributes in the whole codebase, 55 identical icon-only pin buttons per Evaluation view, and 179 of 204 controls on that view under 24 px.
6. On a phone the sidebar becomes a 522 px block of 18 wrapped buttons above a 136 px header, so the H1 sits at y = 697 on every view and every navigation scrolls back to the top of that block.
7. The screens are documentation, not an instrument. Prose is 70 to 87 percent of the words on Liquidity, Benchmarks, Cohorts, Coverage, Analysis Lab, Funnel and De-smoothing, lines run 250 to 266 characters, and the rubric's internal keys (`strategy_match 2/3`, `data_held 0/2`), registry keys (`hl_paf`, `kkr_kpec`), enum values (`managed_assets`) and repository paths (`data/roster_decisions.md`, `docs/verification_queue.md`) render on adviser surfaces in 438, 1,125, 80 and 50-plus places respectively. The `test_surfaces` gate missed all of these because its pattern list is a blocklist of phrases, not a rule.
8. The documents a committee would file are 34 to 37 pages, 57 to 63 percent of which is the Federal Register appendix, with tables that render as equal-width columns in LibreOffice, Google Docs and any PDF pipeline (the widths are written to `tcW` but not to `tblGrid`), no page numbers, no headers, a signature line as the last line after 15 pages of regulation, 58 snake_case tokens in one memo, and no sentence anywhere of the form "On factor (g), the record shows X."
