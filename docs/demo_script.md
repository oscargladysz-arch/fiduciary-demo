# Tark Demo Script — 7 minutes
### Setup: venv active, `streamlit run app.py`, browser on the Anchor Plan view. Fallback: screenshots folder.

**0:00 — Anchor Plan.** "This is a real 401(k) — a ~$570M NY media/technology company plan, from its public Form 5500. Everything you'll see runs against this plan." Point at the liquidity-tail banner: *1,847 separated participants still hold balances — 36% of accounts. Hold that number.*

**1:00 — Candidate Roster.** "Six real products, six different legal wrappers — a tender-offer fund, a true interval fund, two non-traded '34 Act vehicles, and a listed fund that will fail on purpose. Every cell you'll see is traceable to a filing."

**1:45 — Six-Factor Evaluation (hl_paf → Fees tab).** Open cell 2.1's Source expander: *"1.40% — but on Managed Assets, which includes leverage. Same headline rate as StepStone; different base. The tool caught the fee-base trap in the filing's own words."* Switch product to kkr_kpec → Complexity tab: *"K-PEC issues Schedule K-1s — near-disqualifying for a DC recordkeeper. StepStone literally advertises '1099 instead of K-1s' in its prospectus. Same factor, opposite answers, both cited."*

**3:15 — Benchmark Selection (cliffwater_cclfx).** "Here's the moat. Four candidate lanes, a 12-point rubric, and the part nobody else builds — the rejection log." Point at CDLI's row: *"The most strategy-exact index available is published by the fund's own adviser. The engine scored it zero on independence and documented why. BlackRock can't build this feature — they own the yardsticks."* Show KS-PME 1.2519 / +3.21%. Click **Download decision memo** — open the docx: *"Rule language, the six factors, the rejection log, provenance — the artifact a fiduciary actually files."*

**4:45 — Benchmark Selection (dxyz).** "And when no meaningful benchmark exists, the tool says so instead of pretending." Read the escalation. *"Listed at $9, hit $99.79 twelve days later, −90% drawdown, still up 188%. Benchmarking that price benchmarks the premium, not the portfolio — and the memo documents the refusal."*

**5:30 — Liquidity Match (cliffwater_cclfx).** "Remember the 1,847? Here's the match: quarterly 5% repurchases — eight consecutive N-23C3A filings on disk prove the cadence — against this plan's demand profile. Verdict: conditional. Daily 404(c) menus need a bridging structure; the tool names it instead of hand-waving." Switch to breit: *"Conditional-weak — because BREIT's own 10-K admits it prorated repurchases when requests exceeded caps. Capacity on paper has failed in stress before, and the record says so."*

**6:30 — Close.** "Six factors, real filings, a benchmark decision with its rejections, a liquidity verdict, and a memo you can hand to ERISA counsel. The rule's comment period closes this summer; the final rule and the Intel decision land by year-end. This is the workflow layer for that moment."
